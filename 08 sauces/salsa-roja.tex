\begin{quote}
\textit{Toasted guajillo, árbol, and morita chiles are soaked, then blended with air-fried tomatoes, onion, and garlic. The puree is strained and fried in a little oil until glossy, then finished with salt and a little sugar. Makes about 2~cups.}
\end{quote}

\section*{Ingredients}
\setlength{\columnsep}{20pt}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\noindent
    Guajillo chiles \dotfill 4 \\
    Árbol chiles \dotfill 8 \\
    Morita chile \dotfill 1 \\
    Roma tomatoes \dotfill 12 oz. \\
    White onion \dotfill \nicefrac{1}{4} small \\
    \columnbreak
    Garlic cloves \dotfill 3 \\
    Apple cider vinegar \dotfill 2 tsp. \\
    Ground cumin \dotfill \nicefrac{1}{8} tsp. \\
    Neutral oil \dotfill 1 Tbsp. \\
    Kosher salt \dotfill 1 tsp. \\
    Sugar \dotfill \nicefrac{1}{2} tsp. \\
\end{multicols}

\section*{Directions}

\noindent
Preheat the air fryer to \textit{400°F} ---
Stem and seed the \textbf{guajillo chiles} and the \textbf{morita chile}; stem the \textbf{árbol chiles} and leave the seeds in ---
Halve the \textbf{tomatoes}; cut the \textbf{onion} into wedges ---
Measure the \textbf{vinegar}, \textbf{cumin}, \textbf{oil}, \textbf{salt}, and \textbf{sugar}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Toast the \textbf{guajillo chiles} in a dry skillet over medium heat. Press them flat for about \textit{20~seconds} per side, until fragrant and lighter inside. Keep them red-brown. Discard any that smell sharp or acrid, and toast replacements.

    \item In the same skillet, stir the \textbf{árbol chiles} and the \textbf{morita chile} over medium heat until they darken in spots and smell toasty, about \textit{1~minute}. The \textbf{morita} darkens faster. Lift it out as soon as it spots. Discard any chile that smells sharp or acrid, and toast a replacement.

    \item Cover the toasted chiles with hot water. Hold them under a small plate. Soak \textit{15--20~minutes}, until soft and pliable.

    \item While the chiles soak, air-fry the \textbf{tomatoes} and \textbf{onion} at \textit{400°F}. Place the tomato halves cut side up and the onion wedges in one layer. Cook \textit{4--8~minutes}, until the skins begin to blister. Leave about \textit{8~minutes} of cooking still to come.

    \item Add the unpeeled \textbf{garlic cloves}. Cook about \textit{8~minutes} more, \textit{12--16~minutes} in all. The tomato skins should blister and blacken in patches, and the flesh should collapse when pressed. The onion edges should be black, with a soft, sweet center. A garlic clove should squeeze soft from the skin, and the inside should be golden. Continue in \textit{2~minute} increments until the tomatoes collapse and the garlic squeezes soft. Discard a clove that is black inside.

    \item Pour any juice from the air-fryer drawer into the blender.

    \item Peel the \textbf{garlic}.

    \item Lift the chiles out of the soaking water and keep the water.

    \item Blend the chiles, \textbf{tomatoes}, \textbf{onion}, \textbf{garlic}, 2~tsp. \textbf{apple cider vinegar}, \nicefrac{1}{8}~tsp. \textbf{ground cumin}, the drawer juice, and \nicefrac{1}{2}~cup soaking water until completely smooth, about \textit{1--2~minutes}. Add another splash of soaking water when the puree stops moving.

    \item Strain the puree through a fine-mesh strainer. Press hard, until only dry skins remain. Discard the solids.

    \item Heat 1~Tbsp. \textbf{neutral oil} in a small saucepan over medium heat until it shimmers.

    \item Pour in the puree and stand back. It will sputter. Simmer over medium heat, stirring often, for \textit{8--12~minutes}, until the sauce darkens a shade, the raw chile smell is gone, and it coats a spoon but still pours. Set the lid on at an angle between stirs. Stir in a spoonful of soaking water when the sauce thickens past a loose pour.

    \item Take the pan off the heat. Stir in 1~tsp. \textbf{kosher salt} and \nicefrac{1}{2}~tsp. \textbf{sugar}. Taste. The sauce should be spicy, lightly smoky, and a little sweet. Add a pinch more \textbf{salt} or \textbf{sugar} as needed.

    \item Cool until warm or at room temperature. The sauce thickens as it cools. Loosen with a splash of soaking water when it sets past a loose pour. Serve warm or at room temperature. Makes about 2~cups.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Hints and Notes}

\subsection*{Yield}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Makes about 2~cups, enough for a week of tacos.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Mise en Place}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Preheat the air fryer to \textit{400°F} while you stem the chiles and cut the vegetables.
    \item Stem and seed the \textbf{guajillo chiles} and the \textbf{morita chile}. Stem the \textbf{árbol chiles} and leave the seeds in.
    \item Halve the \textbf{tomatoes}. Cut the \textbf{onion} into wedges. Leave the \textbf{garlic} in its skin.
    \item Measure the \textbf{vinegar}, \textbf{cumin}, \textbf{oil}, \textbf{salt}, and \textbf{sugar}.
    \item Toast and start the soak before the vegetables go into the air fryer. The soak covers the roast.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Ingredient Tips}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Choose \textbf{guajillo chiles} that bend rather than snap. Seed them. The seeds taste bitter.
    \item Heat sits in the \textbf{árbol} seeds. Shake out half the seeds for a milder sauce, or add 2 or 3 more chiles for a hotter one.
    \item Use one dried \textbf{morita}, the small sweet smoked chipotle. A chipotle meco is harsher smoke.
    \item Ripe red \textbf{Roma tomatoes} supply the sweetness. Pale tomatoes stay sharp.
    \item \textbf{Neutral oil} means safflower, canola, or grapeseed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Preparation Tips}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Toast only until the chiles smell fragrant. Black, acrid chiles will make the whole batch bitter.
    \item Save the juice in the air-fryer drawer. It is sweet, and the sauce needs it.
    \item Press the puree hard through the strainer. Skins left in the sauce taste gritty.
    \item The puree spatters when it hits the hot oil. Stir, and set the lid on at an angle between stirs.
    \item Salt and sugar go in after the simmer so the reduction does not make them too strong.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Make Ahead \& Storage}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cool, then refrigerate in a jar up to \textit{2~weeks}.
    \item Oil rises as it sits. Stir before serving.
    \item The sauce is best warm or at room temperature. Straight from the fridge, the chile taste is muted.
    \item It thickens when cold. Loosen with a spoonful of water.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Serving Suggestions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spoon it on tacos, fried eggs, beans, and grilled meat.
    \item A small spoonful is enough. The heat builds.
\end{itemize}
